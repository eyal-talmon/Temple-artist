Burning Man 2027 · Black Rock City, Nevada

A tree turned upside down, to hold what we carry.

The 2027 Burning Man Temple is being called simply The Temple, leaving room for the crew and community to bring their own meaning to it.

01 Design Inspiration

Patterns that repeat at every scale.

Trees, rivers
& their tributaries

Nature is full of patterns that repeat at different scales. These forms follow basic mathematical principles to organize themselves beautifully and efficiently.

Zoom in or out far enough on any form or ecosystem and the pattern appears. Trees show it in their branching; rivers and their tributaries do too. This Temple explores those patterns to show how participants are connected through one powerful, common experience.

i. Ten years on

Made from trees, speaking through trees.

2027 marks ten years since Steve co-led the 2017 Temple, which also used trees as its philosophical driver. There’s something about trees that makes them a strong center for the Temple. Perhaps it’s because the Temple is ultimately made from them, and they find expression as a vehicle for healing.

As a tree splits from trunk to boughs, branches and twigs, the form repeats, so the whole mirrors itself at every scale. The hope is that the Temple holds that same quality: a collective grieving, each part reflecting the whole.

ii. The turn

Stuck, until it was flipped upside down.

The first branching diagram made a tree: trunk at the base, canopy on top. But the shape was unstable and didn’t read as a Temple. Turning the concept upside down made it work. That was the final step from tree to Tree‑Temple.

02 This Temple is an Experiment

Is the Temple itself one level of a larger fractal structure, one we can’t easily see?

Fractal branching is not only the shape of the Temple; it’s a philosophical experiment. If in fractal branching each branch reflects the greater whole, then a change at one level should echo across the whole as well.

What happens when the form of the Temple aligns with that unseen structure?
